0.Abstract

Four interactive world models shipped within weeks of each other. Every one of them was evaluated on fidelity, temporal consistency, and controllability. These are admirable properties. They are not why you clicked the demo. You clicked the demo because you wanted to walk around in it.

We present WORLD BENCH, the first benchmark to rank world models by fun per parameter (FPP): the amount of measured human enjoyment a system delivers, divided by the number of parameters it had to train to deliver it. We evaluate four 2026 systems - Atlas (World Labs), GWM Worlds 2 (Runway), H3-World (MiniMax-H3), and SolarWM (Wan2.2) - on a standardized battery of walking-around tasks, and we report the results with a straight face.1

2.Methodology

All evaluations were conducted on a single consumer laptop, in a browser, the way you were going to do it anyway. No special hardware was provisioned. No snacks were consumed in the hour before rating, to control for confounding joy.

Task battery. Each model was driven through four standardized tasks:

  1. T1 · Corridor Walk. The rater holds W for sixty seconds. Scored on the urge to keep holding W.
  2. T2 · Door Finding. A door exists somewhere in the latent space. The rater attempts to reach it. Scored on whether the door, upon approach, is still a door.
  3. T3 · Vibe Traversal. Unstructured exploration. Scored on the number of times the rater says "oh, whoa" involuntarily.
  4. T4 · Camera Drift Endurance. The rater releases all keys and watches the world continue without them. Scored on existential texture.

Rater panel. Nine raters: eight graduate students and one dog owner. All raters reported prior exposure to at least one video game. Raters were instructed to have fun and were timed.4

Metrics. The composite FUN score (0-10) aggregates task scores with equal weighting, normalized against a control condition (a static JPEG of a hallway). Fun per parameter is then:

FPP = FUN / max(Ptrained, ε)   ·   reported as FUN/B (fun units per billion trained parameters)
REPRODUCIBILITY STATEMENTH3-World and SolarWM release weights and data; their scores are reproducible. Atlas and GWM Worlds 2 release vibes; their scores are not. We encourage all labs to disclose parameter counts so that they may be ranked by a parody benchmark with full scientific rigor.

3.Limitations

Fun is subjective. The sample size is small. The hallway JPEG was, in hindsight, a strong control and scored 1.2, which raises questions about the hallway. The dog owner was unavailable for comment. One rater attempted to add "the smell of rain in GWM Worlds 2" as a scoring dimension; this was rejected, then quietly adopted, then rejected again. We do not report inter-rater reliability because it would weaken the paper.
